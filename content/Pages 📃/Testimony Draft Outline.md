Here’s a clear, heartfelt outline you can use to write your Christian testimony. It follows the classic “before → how → after” testimony structure while incorporating the specific phases of your life you mentioned. Feel free to expand each section with your personal stories, feelings, and Scripture verses that were meaningful to you.

### Christian Testimony Outline

#### I. Introduction (Hook + Brief Overview)
- A short, attention-grabbing opening sentence or moment (e.g., “For years I walked away from the God I once knew as a child…”)
- One-sentence summary of your overall journey: Catholic childhood → drifted away in teens/early adulthood → powerful return to faith as an adult.

#### II. Part 1: My Upbringing in the Catholic Church (The “Before” – Life with religion but perhaps without deep personal relationship)
- Early childhood memories of faith
  - Family life, attending Mass, Catholic school (if applicable), sacraments (Baptism, First Communion, Confession, Confirmation)
  - What you were taught about God, Jesus, sin, salvation
  - How faith felt at the time (routine? comforting? fear-based? genuine but shallow?)
- Positive aspects you’re thankful for now (foundation, moral framework, reverence for the Eucharist, etc.)
- Hints of what was missing (if any): Was it more cultural/traditional than a living relationship with Christ?

#### III. Part 2: Teenage Years and Early Adulthood – Walking Away
- When and why the drift began (high school, college, influences, questions, hurts, etc.)
- Key events or influences that pulled you away
  - Doubts about Church teaching or God’s existence
  - Desire for freedom, peer pressure, lifestyle choices
  - Disappointment with Catholics/Christians you knew, scandals in the Church, etc.
- How life looked during this season
  - What you chased instead of God (relationships, success, partying, independence, etc.)
  - The emptiness, consequences, or restlessness you eventually felt (even if you didn’t recognize it as spiritual hunger at the time)

#### IV. Part 3: The Turning Point – How I Came Back
- The circumstances that led to your return
  - A crisis, loss, rock-bottom moment?
  - An invitation from a person, a homily, a book, a retreat (e.g., Alpha, Life in the Spirit seminar, confession after years away)?
  - A Scripture verse, a prayer answered, an encounter with Christ in Adoration or at Mass?
- The moment (or process) of surrender and conversion
  - Realizing you needed Jesus personally, not just “religion”
  - Experiencing God’s mercy, especially in the Sacrament of Reconciliation
  - Understanding salvation by grace through faith more deeply

#### V. Part 4: Life Now – Walking with Christ as an Adult
- How your faith is different now than in childhood
  - Personal relationship with Jesus, not just rituals
  - Deeper love for the sacraments, Scripture, prayer life
  - Involvement in parish or ministries (if applicable)
- Specific ways God has changed you (character, priorities, relationships, peace, purpose)
- Challenges you still face and how faith helps you through them
- Your hope for the future and trust in God’s plan

#### VI. Conclusion
- A clear statement of what Jesus means to you today
- A brief invitation or encouragement to the reader/listener (e.g., “No matter how far you’ve walked away, God is waiting with open arms—just like He was for me.”)
- Optional closing Scripture verse (common ones: Luke 15:20 – the Prodigal Son, 2 Corinthians 5:17, Ephesians 2:8–9, Psalm 34:18, etc.)
- End with gratitude: “To God be the glory.”

This outline keeps your story chronological while highlighting the transformation, which is the heart of every strong testimony. Aim for 800–1500 words (about 5–10 minutes when spoken). Be honest, specific, and focus on Jesus more than on dramatic sin details—people connect most with the hope and mercy you found.
