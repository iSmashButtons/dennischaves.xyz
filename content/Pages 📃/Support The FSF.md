The Free Software Foundation (FSF) fights for **software freedom** — the idea that you (and everyone else) should have full control over the software and devices you use every day. For regular people — not just programmers or tech experts — supporting the FSF (through donations, membership, or simply spreading the word) directly protects your privacy, security, and independence in an increasingly digital world.

Here’s why it matters to everyday users in 2025:

| Everyday Problem | How Proprietary Software Makes It Worse | How FSF/Free Software Fixes It | Why Your Support Helps |
|------------------|-----------------------------------------|--------------------------------|------------------------|
| Your phone/computer spies on you | Apps and OSes from Apple, Google, Microsoft collect your data, track your location, and sell it | Free software (e.g., GNU/Linux phones, Replicant, Libreboot laptops) lets you see and remove tracking code | FSF endorses fully free distros and hardware | Funds campaigns that pressure companies and educate people about alternatives |
| Companies remotely disable or brick your devices | John Deere tractors, Apple iPhones, even printers can be locked down or crippled by the manufacturer | With free software you can fix, modify, or keep using old devices running forever (Right to Repair wins) | FSF runs the “Fight to Repair” and “Defective by Design” campaigns against DRM |
| You’re locked into expensive subscriptions & cloud services | Adobe, Microsoft 365, Spotify, etc. — pay forever or lose access to your own files | Tools like GIMP (Photoshop replacement), LibreOffice, VLC, FreeCAD stay yours forever, no subscription | FSF created and defends the **GPL license** that keeps Linux, Android (partly), WordPress, etc. free for everyone |
| AI and “smart” devices are black boxes that control you | ChatGPT, Alexa, Tesla autopilot | Closed models can censor, discriminate, or refuse service; you can’t audit them | FSF pushes for fully free AI and fights non-free JavaScript that traps you on websites | Your donation keeps the only major organization loudly demanding free AI and web standards |
| Governments and corporations push surveillance laws | “Chat control,” digital IDs, mandatory backdoors in apps | Free software is the only practical way to have verifiable private communication (e.g., Signal server is free software) | FSF is one of the few groups that consistently opposes surveillance legislation worldwide |
| Kids learn on locked-down Chromebooks/iPads in school | Schools teach dependence on Google/Apple/Microsoft from age 5 | Free software lets students (and teachers) actually learn how computers work instead of just how to click corporate buttons | FSF works to get free software into education and fights proprietary ed-tech |

### Tangible things the FSF has done that benefit regular people (even if you’ve never heard of them):
- Created the **GNU General Public License (GPL)** → the “viral” license that powers ~80 % of the servers running the Internet, Android phones, WordPress sites, etc.
- Runs the **Free Software Directory** (17 000+ fully free programs) and **Respects Your Freedom** hardware certification so you can buy laptops/phones that don’t spy on you.
- Successfully fought software patents in Europe and helped kill bad laws in the US.
- Keeps GNU tools (bash, gcc, coreutils…) alive — the invisible foundation of macOS, Linux, Android, and most cloud services.
- In 2025 they’re actively working on **mobile phone freedom** projects and hosting hackathons for fully free alternatives.

### The blunt reality
Most “open source” groups today focus on making corporations happy (cheaper development, faster innovation). The FSF is almost the only major organization that still puts **ordinary users’ freedom first**, even when it’s unpopular or inconvenient. Without the FSF’s stubborn defense of copyleft and user rights, a lot of today’s “free” software would have quietly become proprietary again (it’s already happening with some AI models and cloud services).

Supporting the FSF (even $5–10/month as an associate member) is basically buying insurance against a future where you don’t own anything you buy and every click is watched and monetized.

If that future scares you — or you just like the fact that tools like VLC, Firefox, Linux, 7-Zip, and FreeCAD exist and will always be free — then the FSF is worth backing.

https://www.fsf.org/associate/support_freedom (membership starts at $10/month or $5 for students)
