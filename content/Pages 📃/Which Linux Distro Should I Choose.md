# Intro
- How long I've been a Linux user depends on when you start counting. My first attempts at using Linux as my OS go back to at least 2003, maybe further.
- I think my experience, from my first failed attempts to daily user of about a decade, has taught me at least one lesson about switching to Linux and choosing a distro.

## My first attempts
- High school, mIRC. Look, mom! I'm a hacker!
	- RHEL
	- OpenSuSE
	- Mandrake
	- Falling for the Gentoo meme.
- Giving up: I'm not actually good at computers and I'm not smart enough to learn.

## Gone but not forgotten
- While living in a trash apartment, my MacBook Pro was stolen by a weed dealer and aspiring rapper.
- To replace it, I got a $300 Dell netbook. At the time Ubuntu was becoming very popular and Dell offered it as an OS option.

## Reunited and never parting
- After the Pi made me comfortable with Linux, I purchased a used laptop and installed Ubuntu on it.
- Around 2017, after a Windows 10 update produced some frustrating results. I decided I had learned enough about Linux that I could use it for everything, every day.
- I spent the next few years changing distributions at least twice per year.
	- Ubuntu and it's flavors. *boring*

		- Arch/Manjaro: too hot!

## The best distro may be no distro
- First determine if there are any deal-breakers.
	- Are you a gamer? 
		- Linux has great performance for games. 
		- Bleeding-edge hardware may not have driver support.
		- But if you are unwilling to give up the online multi-player games that require kernel-level anti-cheat programs, Linux may not be a good choice for your next OS.
	- Are you the owner of a business? 
		- MS Office and most specialized business software doesn't run on Linux. 
		- It's not impossible, and many people do it, but are you one of them?
	- Are you unwilling or unable to compromise on proprietary software?
		- Most popular proprietary applications do not run on Linux and are unlikely to ever run on Linux in the future.
		- If you are not willing to use GIMP instead of Photoshop.


- What kind of user are you?
- What do you want to get out of your computer?
- The most important factor is whether you are a casual user or someone who has an interest in computers.

## The Casual User

- Describe the casual user:
	- Isn't interested in how anything works under the hood.
	- Their idea of customizing their computer is changing the background image.
	- Most frequently used application is Google Chrome. 
	- Availability of other applications may be a deal-breaker. Any professional or even semi-professional photographer is not going to trade years of experience with Adobe Photoshop for GIMP. 
- Distro recommendation: Fedora Silverblue or Kinoite.
	- The best "just works" operating systems for people who really want their computer to "just work."
	- The only difference is the desktop environment. Choose which one looks best to you. Silverblue's desktop is more like a tablet or Mac OS, while Kinoite is closer to a traditional Windows desktop.
	- Immutable core image keeps users or programs from messing with anything.
	- Updates are automatic and seamless. My father-in-law's laptop has updated several times and he hasn't even noticed.

## The Computer Enthusiast
- Describe the enthusiast:
	- Has a high-level understanding of hardware and software.
	- Likes to poke and tinker, see what new things he can do.
	- Has probably built a PC from parts and installed an OS before.
	- Frequently uses programs other than a web browser.
	- Has some history of using a command-line environment, or at least isn't afraid of one.
	- Doesn't mind something breaking once in a while, so long as it's not essential.
	- Still has an expectation of general stability.
- Recommended distro: it doesn't matter
	- Because you know enough about what you're doing, I don't think there is anything I can tell you to avoid you trying several distributions.
	- And you probably should, to find the one that fits just right.
- My advice to you: dig down and understand the system
	- Learn to use the terminal. It's essential to know at least the basics for when your tinkering eventually leads you into trouble.
