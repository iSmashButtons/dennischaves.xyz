I'm a CNC machinist in the Polymer Seals division at A.W. Chesterton, and I've never been satisfied with just “running the program.” I like to dig into the tool paths, the G-code, the macros, sometimes right down to the PLC — anything that helps me understand and perfect the process. That curiosity has turned me into the go-to problem solver, trainer, and drives everything I build, both at work and at home.

## My Professional Journey
I started my career at United Electric Controls in Watertown, MA. There, in addition to a fast and hands-on introduction to CNC machining, I also had in-depth training and practical experience with Lean Manufacturing and The Toyota Way. United Electric is a Shingo Prize winning company that takes Lean Manufacturing very seriously.

At M&K Engineering, I learned to program lathes the hard way: by punching 
g-code directly into the control, with a machine manual on my bench. During my time at this company, I gained experience operating a wide variety of CNC machine tools including Swiss-type lathes, vertical mills, and wire EDM. I also learned to use SolidWorks and learned much more about GD & T during my time at this company.

A.W. Chesterton is where I was able to take my skills into a leadership role. I started off as a humble operator, but quickly rose through the ranks to become a lead hand. In my 5th year at the company, I was promoted to supervisor. However, after one year in the role, I realized my talents were far more technical than interpersonal. I resigned and returned to the floor while another lead hand took the position of supervisor. Everyone was happier with this arrangement. 

In 2026 I was promoted again. This time, to a role that much better fits my skills. I currently work for the Manufacturing Engineering team as a Machining Services Specialist. I support the CNC operators with troubleshooting, work on projects to improve efficiency and quality, advise our design and applications engineers about what can and cannot be done in our shop, program all our CNC equipment, design fixturing and tooling, and other important tasks. I usually summarize my new role like this: I'm finally in a position to fix everything I've been complaining about since 2017.

## The Personal Side
I’m a first-generation American, son of Portuguese immigrants. I'm a city kid at heart, born in raised in Somerville, but moved to New Hampshire to start my family. At home I’m husband to an incredible wife and dad to two energetic daughters whose names come from our favorite movies (yes, really). Being a “girl dad” and a maker naturally collide — I once designed and printed a little hot glue gun mold so they could finish their bead bracelets with a strong, permanent connection, instead of knots. Still one of my proudest prints.

## Continuing Education and Growth
I did complete some college courses before starting my career in manufacturing. These days however, between work and home life, it's hard to find time to sleep, let alone obtain a degree. Still, I press myself to learn and grow through online resources. Lately I've been chewing my way through [Elements of Mechanical Design](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-72-elements-of-mechanical-design-spring-2009/) from MIT's OpenCourseWare. And brushing up on my math using [Khan Academy](https://khanacademy.org).

In 2026, I have plans to add to my CAM skill set by getting further training on EdgeCAM software. I'm excited about EdgeCAM's use of JavaScript in creating macros. This is the perfect opportunity to combine my interest in computer programming with my experience in he machine shop.
